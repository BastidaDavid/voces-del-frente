Wallace C. Strobel

101st Airborne Division News
June 2, 1944

Wallace C. Strobel, whose face inspires our fictional character, appears in the preparations before D-Day. In this experience, his figure allows us to respectfully explore the memory of thousands of young people who faced uncertainty and fear in World War II.

His image captures a powerful human tension: the moment before everything changes. That threshold — between the calm of the bunker and the roar outside — opens a narrative territory to talk about responsibility, camaraderie, and the weight of decisions.

The interview seeks to avoid the spectacle of combat to focus on the intimate: routines, objects, voices, and breathing. The helmet pressing, the crackling radio, a folded letter in the pocket; small anchors so that the memory is believable and close.

It is also a pedagogical opportunity: to show how immersive journalism can build empathy without glorifying war, using sound, spatial resources, and a 3D staging that accompanies — but does not replace — the testimony.

In short, interviewing him — even through an inspired character — allows us to speak of peace from those who knew its fragility. That the audience listens, situates themselves, and above all, wonders about the seams that hold life together.
